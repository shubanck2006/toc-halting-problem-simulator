Academic & Syllabus Alignment

This project covers the core requirements of Module 5: Turing Machines and Undecidability:

  • Formal Definition: 7-tuple $M = (Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, \delta, q_0, B, F)$ with expandable bidirectional tape.
  • Instantaneous Descriptions (IDs): $u q v \vdash u' p v'$ step-by-step state tracking.
  • Language Acceptance: Precise distinction between halting acceptance, rejection, and infinite loops.
  • Universal Turing Machines: Binary/unary encoding of states, transitions, and tape inputs into $\langle M, w \rangle$.
  • Recursive vs Recursively Enumerable: Side-by-side verification of deciders vs recognizers.
  • The Halting Problem ($H_{\text{TM}}$): Diagonalization proof and self-referential paradox.
  • Additional Theorems: Church-Turing thesis, Cantor countability, Rice's theorem, and Post Correspondence Problem (PCP).

Implementation Safeguards

No False Halting Claims:

In accordance with theoretical limits, our simulator never claims to "solve" the general halting problem. The universal halting detector is modeled as a hypothetical construct to demonstrate its mathematical impossibility.

Step Limits vs Non-Termination:

When an execution exceeds the safety threshold (e.g. 1,000 steps), the system explicitly reports STEP_LIMIT_REACHED, never erroneously asserting that the machine definitely loops forever.

Mathematical Rigor:

The tape is dynamically unbounded in both directions, transition lookup follows strict deterministic rules, and state diagram generation is dynamically rendered from the machine's transition graph.
